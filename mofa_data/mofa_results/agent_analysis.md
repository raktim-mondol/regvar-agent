### Detailed Analysis of Top Candidates

#### 🔴 TIER 1: Strongest Candidates — High Confidence, Prostate-Relevant Mechanism

---

**1. chr17:48728343:C>T (rs138213197) — OR = 3.85 | HOXB13**

The strongest combined signal. This variant maps near **HOXB13**, a cardinal prostate lineage transcription factor. The alternate allele (T) is predicted to *reduce* HOXB13 and MIR3185 expression across diverse cell types (mesangial cells, Caki2 renal carcinoma, bronchus fibroblasts, mesenchymal stem cells — quantile scores up to −0.99980). The OR of 3.85 is the highest in the panel by a large margin.

> **Predicted mechanism:** The variant disrupts a regulatory element controlling HOXB13, reducing its expression in stromal and epithelial compartments. Given that HOXB13 is a pioneer factor in prostate identity and is recurrently mutated in prostate cancer (G84E), expression-level perturbation in the tumour microenvironment could alter AR-driven transcription and stromal-epithelial crosstalk.

**2. chr10:46046326:A>G (rs10993994) — OR = 1.23 | MSMB**

The canonical prostate cancer risk locus. AlphaGenome confirms a massive RNA-seq signal concentrated on **MSMB** (microseminoprotein-beta, PSP94) — effects span hepatocytes, T-cells, keratinocytes, bronchial epithelium, mammary cells, and cancer lines (MCF-7, HT-29, HepG2, A549). The G allele is the risk allele; prediction shows both positive and negative raw scores depending on cell type, consistent with context-dependent regulatory effects. This variant lies within the MSMB promoter and is known to affect an ARE (androgen response element).

> **Predicted mechanism:** The risk allele alters MSMB promoter activity, reducing MSMB secretion from prostate epithelial cells. Loss of MSMB, a tumour-suppressive secretory protein, promotes a permissive stromal niche. This is the single best-characterised prostate cancer regulatory variant — AlphaGenome predictions are concordant with the known biology, providing internal validation.

### Important Caveats

1. **AlphaGenome predictions are *in silico* hypotheses.** They have not been validated against your lab's specific assays and should be treated as experimental leads, not conclusions.

2. **RNA-seq dominance.** Most top effects are RNA-seq predictions. This reflects the training data distribution of AlphaGenome. Absence of ATAC-seq or Hi-C predictions for a given variant does *not* mean those mechanisms are absent — the model may simply lack power in those modalities for that locus/context.

3. **Tissue specificity.** The ontology constraint (UBERON:0002367, prostate gland) was applied, but many top biosamples are non-prostate (hepatocytes, T-cells, cancer lines). This reflects AlphaGenome's reference panel composition. Effects seen in other tissues can still be informative if the regulatory element is active in prostate but the model's prostate biosample coverage is thinner.

4. **Variant linkage.** Many of these variants tag larger haplotype blocks. The causal variant may differ from the scored SNP; fine-mapping or saturation mutagenesis of the region would be needed for causal resolution.

5. **The chr8:127091872 variant (rs183373024) with OR=2.91 and prostate-specific ChIP-seq histone effects is the single most actionable finding** — it has multi-assay support in disease-relevant lines and maps to one of the most intensively studied prostate cancer risk loci (8q24). I recommend prioritising Experiment 1.
